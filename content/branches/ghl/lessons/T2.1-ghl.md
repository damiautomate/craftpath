You already did the hard part. In the previous step you produced a **configuration plan** — the process a client actually runs, broken into stages, with the fields you'd need to capture at each one. Nothing was open yet; it was pure design. Now you turn that plan into a real, working setup.

The video is a full walkthrough of setting up the CRM inside GoHighLevel: building a pipeline, naming stages, and adding opportunities. Watch it once end-to-end, then do the build yourself using **your own plan**, not the example in the video.

## What to watch for

The walkthrough moves fast and uses the presenter's example business. As you watch, keep translating what they show back to the decisions you already made:

- **Where the pipeline lives.** In GoHighLevel, pipelines sit under Opportunities. This is the object your whole plan maps onto — one pipeline per distinct process you designed.
- **Stages are your process, not theirs.** The presenter names stages for their business. Yours come from your plan. If your client's process was "enquiry → site visit → quote sent → won/lost," that's what your stages should read, verbatim. Don't inherit someone else's funnel.
- **Where custom fields attach.** Notice the moment they add or reference a field on a contact or opportunity. Every field in your plan needs a home here — and every field you *didn't* plan is clutter you'll regret. Add only what your plan calls for.
- **Won and Lost are automatic.** GoHighLevel adds these two closing stages for you, so don't design them as manual stages — leave room for them at the end.

## Your task

Working from your configuration plan, build the real thing:

1. **Create one pipeline** named for the process you designed.
2. **Add the stages from your plan, in order,** using your plan's wording. Resist adding stages the plan didn't justify.
3. **Add one custom field** that your plan identified as needed but that isn't there by default (for example, "lead source" or "budget range").
4. **Create one test opportunity** and move it through a stage, so you can see the board behave the way your plan intended.

## Watch out for

- **Copying the video's stages instead of your plan's.** The whole point of the trunk step was to design for a specific process. If your pipeline looks like the tutorial's, you skipped the thinking.
- **Over-building.** More stages and more fields feel productive but make the CRM harder to use and harder to hand to a client. If your plan didn't call for it, don't add it yet.
- **Naming stages after outcomes you can't observe.** A stage should represent something a real person can look at and confirm ("quote sent"), not a hope ("interested").

When your pipeline mirrors your plan and a test opportunity flows through it, capture the proof below. That screenshot is the first piece of evidence that you can take a client's process and stand it up in a real tool — which is exactly what they pay for.
